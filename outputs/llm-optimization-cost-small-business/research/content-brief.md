## Target query

**Primary:** `how much does LLM optimization cost for small businesses`
**Head term:** `LLM optimization cost`

### Terminology note (important for the writer)

The market uses four near-synonyms for the same service, and the article must
say so explicitly in the first section or readers arriving on any of the four
terms will bounce:

| Term | Expansion | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| LLMO / LLM optimization | Large Language Model Optimization | Newest; least standardised |
| GEO | Generative Engine Optimization | Most common in agency pricing pages |
| AEO | Answer Engine Optimization | Common; slight emphasis on answer boxes |
| AI SEO / AI search optimization | — | Broadest, often a marketing wrapper |

Pricing data is published mostly under **GEO** and **AEO**, so the article must
bridge the vocabulary to use that data honestly.

## Search intent

**Commercial investigation.** The searcher is a small-business owner or
marketing lead deciding whether to buy, what to budget, and whom to buy from.
They are not looking for a definition of LLMs — they want numbers.

Secondary intent: **price validation** — many arrive holding a quote and want
to know if $2,500/month is reasonable.

**What that means:** lead with numbers, not education. A page that spends 400
words defining generative engines before quoting a price loses to one that
opens with the range.

---

## SERP characteristics

Ten of the top results are agency-published pricing guides. Observations:

- **Format that ranks:** long-form pricing guide, 1,500–3,000 words, with a
  tier table (DIY / freelancer / agency / enterprise) and an FAQ block.
- **Title pattern:** `<Service> Cost in 2026: Pricing Guide` — the year appears
  in nearly every ranking title. It is a freshness signal in this SERP.
- **Bias to be aware of:** essentially every ranking page is written by an
  agency selling the service. All numbers skew toward justifying a retainer,
  and almost none tell a reader when *not* to buy. That is the gap.
- **No calculators, no neutral sources.** No consumer-advice or trade-press
  page ranks. There is no ".org" or research-backed voice in this SERP.

## Recommended format and depth

- **Format:** pricing guide with a tier comparison table + decision framework
- **Word count:** 1,800–2,200 (competitor median is ~1,800; beat it on
  specificity, not padding)
- **Must include:** one pricing table, one cost-driver list, one FAQ block,
  a "who should not buy this yet" section
- **Reading level:** grade 8–10

## The content gap (write to this)

Every ranking page is an agency pricing page. Not one of them:

1. **Says when not to buy.** A business with no website content, no Google
   Business Profile, and no reviews should fix those first — a GEO retainer on
   a thin site burns money. Nobody says this.
2. **Separates real net-new cost from repackaged SEO.** Much of what is sold as
   "LLM optimization" is schema markup, FAQ content and citation building —
   work an existing SEO retainer may already cover. Readers are at genuine risk
   of paying twice, and no ranking page warns them.
3. **Gives a first-year total, not a monthly figure.** Readers budget annually;
   pages quote monthly.
4. **Prices the free tier honestly.** Several high-value actions (schema, FAQ
   pages, Business Profile, review generation) cost nothing but time.

The article should be the one page in this SERP that a reader could act on
*against* the seller's interest. That is also what makes it citable by AI
engines, which have no reason to prefer another agency's pitch.
